## Article-pipeline candidate, 2026-10-04: "My hourly cron ran 19% of the time"

Proposed by the weekly article-pipeline run. NOT staged, NOT drafted to dev.to. Next free
weekly slot after the queue is **2026-12-16 (Wed)**. Corrected 2026-10-05: this read

**Claim.** A GitHub Actions cron declared hourly delivered 19% of its slots; the daily one never
missed but was 4 to 7 hours late every day. The expression in the YAML is not the schedule.

**Evidence, re-measured 2026-10-04 ~10:05 ET via `gh run list --json createdAt,event`, schedule events only:**
- `cadence-read.yml`, `23 * * * *`: 26 runs over 133.4h (09-28 23:50Z to 10-04 13:16Z) = 0.187/h, **19% of slots**. Gaps min 3.0h, median 5.7h, max 7.3h.
- `publish.yml`, `4 12 * * *`: **10 of 10 days** (09-24 to 10-03), late by 3.8 to 7.3h, median 5.1h.
- Consequence 1: told a collaborator a run would land at 15:23 UTC from the expression; measured median put it near 19:00 (source: memory note feedback_github_cron_delivers_a_fraction, 10-03; not independently re-checkable, cite as anecdote).
- Consequence 2: `tools/cadence_read.py:40` `MIN_GAP_MINUTES = 45` guards against over-sampling an hourly cron; against real ~6h gaps it never binds, so design and series differ by ~8x.
- Consequence 3: two crons an hour apart (draft push 11:04, publisher 12:04) have overlapping jitter windows, so ordering is a coin flip. Fixed in `61595d7` (devto-drafts moved to `4 2 * * *`, ten hours clear).

**Why a hiring manager cares.** Measuring the infrastructure before trusting its declared contract, then changing the design (disjoint windows, sample windows sized in delivered samples) instead of just noting it.

**Headlines.** (a) "My hourly cron ran 19% of the time" (b) "The cron expression is a request, not a schedule" (c) "Daily never missed. It was also never on time."

**Hook.** "On October 3rd I told someone a job would run at 15:23 UTC because the YAML said `23 * * * *`. I had never checked. Here is what 133 hours of run history says."

**Beats.**
1. The table: declared vs delivered, both workflows.
2. Shape of the failure: daily is reliable but late; sub-daily is mostly dropped. An hourly cron here is a ~6-hourly one and nothing in the repo says so.
3. What it broke: two crons an hour apart race; the fix was disjoint windows, not more headroom.
4. What it broke quietly: a sampling guard sized for the declared rate never fires; why the cadence read moved from T+48h to T+14d (a +/-3h error is 6% of a 48h point, under 1% of 14d).
5. Rule: quote the measured history, never a time derived from the expression. One-line `gh run list` recipe.

**Honest caveat (required).** One public repo, ~5.5 days of hourly data and 10 days of daily. GitHub's own docs already warn that scheduled runs can be delayed or dropped under load, so the news is the size of the gap on this repo, not that it exists. Do not generalise the 19% to GitHub at large. Re-run the measurement on publish day and update the numbers.
