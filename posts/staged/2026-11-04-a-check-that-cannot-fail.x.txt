1/ Audited the test counts across 10 of my repos.

Recorded: 290. Actual: 683 passing.

9 of 10 per-repo figures were wrong.

2/ The arithmetic isn't the problem.

I had automated checks whose entire job was catching this. 28 of 97 of them had recorded their
own error message as their expected result.

3/ A command that fails, with the failure saved as the baseline, matches that baseline forever.

It reports agreement. Every single run.

One had been pinning a repo's HEAD to a date three months stale.

4/ Three smaller versions of the same shape, same day:

A build tool printed "up-to-date", skipped the test task, served a cached report. I parsed it
and got the right answer for the wrong reason.

5/ A suite with an optional dep fails at COLLECTION on a base install. Reports zero tests rather
than failing.

Nothing fails when nothing runs.

6/ A badge said 198 where execution says 361.

7/ Nothing crashed. Every signal was green.

The green wasn't false. It was empty.

8/ Then I wrote a check to count the broken checks. Took three tries.

Attempt 1 matched the pattern in its own prose.
Attempt 2 matched it inside its own command.
Attempt 3 finally excluded itself.

9/ Attempt 4 was the best one.

Repairing the last file drove the count to zero. `grep -c` exits non-zero on zero matches.

So it went red at the exact moment it should have gone green, and would have become the 28th
instance of the thing it documents.

10/ The question I ask now isn't "does this pass".

It's "what would make this go red, and has it ever gone red?"

11/ Which is hard for monitors, because a monitor can't fail on clean data by design. You can't
mutation-test it like a function.

Pulling the predicate out and testing the predicate is the best I've got. Not sure it's enough.
