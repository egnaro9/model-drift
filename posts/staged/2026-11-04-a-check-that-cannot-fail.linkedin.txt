version (post 3 to 5 days after the dev.to thread)

I audited the test counts across ten of my repositories this week. Recorded total: 290. Actual:
683 passing. Nine of ten per-repo figures were wrong.

The miss that bothers me is not the arithmetic. It is that I had automated checks whose job was
to catch exactly this, and 28 of 97 of them had recorded their own error message as their
expected result.

A command that fails, with its failure saved as the baseline, matches that baseline forever. It
reports agreement on every run. It had been doing that long enough to pin one repository's state
to a date three months out of date.

Three smaller versions of the same shape, all from the same day:

- A build tool printed "up-to-date", skipped the test task, and served a cached report. I parsed
  it and got the right answer for the wrong reason.
- A suite with an optional dependency fails at collection on a base install, so it reports zero
  tests rather than failing. Nothing fails when nothing runs.
- A badge said 198 where execution says 361.

Nothing here crashed. Every signal was green, and the green was empty rather than false.

The question I am taking forward: not "does this check pass", but "what would make it go red,
and has it ever gone red?"

If you run monitors, how do you test that yours can still fail? A monitor cannot fail on clean
data, so the usual mutation approach does not apply. Extracting the predicate and testing that
is the best I have, and I am not convinced it is enough.
